## Permission authoring: the model reads, code validates and renders — 2026-09-25

Where natural language is turned into an enforceable rule. This changes the **authoring** path only;
the decision path is untouched.

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-045 | **The model reads the customer's words; deterministic code validates, renders and freezes.** The compiler's text parsing stops being the gate on what becomes a rule. The model reads the instruction in any language and returns a structured rule; the registry validates it (the field exists, the operator is one that field permits, the value's type and magnitude are legal); a renderer generates the sentence the customer approves **from the rule object**, never from model prose; and the customer's agreement to that sentence is what creates the rule. This replaces the earlier double reading, where a candidate survived only if the deterministic compiler independently derived the same rule from the same words. Chosen over extending the compiler's grammar to German, French and Italian: measured on 2026-09-25, `höchstens CHF 50 pro Bestellung`, `au plus CHF 50 par commande` and `al massimo CHF 50 per ordine` each produce **no rule at all**, free-text answers such as "only electronics" are refused, and `buy me a jacket, at most CHF 120 this week` is unreadable as a whole — so the grammar excludes three of four Swiss national languages and most ordinary phrasing, and multiplying patterns per language on the authority path is exactly where a misread mints permission the customer never gave. The trade is explicit and one-sided: human review catches what the model **invents** (a wrong value or field is visible in the rendered sentence and rejected) and does not catch what the model **omits**. Two compensating controls are therefore required, not optional — the review names every registry field left unrestricted (LEASH-146), and omission checking (LEASH-155) stops being an optional enhancement. The `says` verbatim-quote requirement **stays a gate**: a rule must still quote, word for word, something the customer actually typed, in the turn it is attributed to, and that quote must carry the rule's value. Relaxing it was tried on 2026-09-25 and reverted — with the gate open a model can quote a background profile line ("prefers shops with returns") and turn it into a candidate rule, which DEC-034 forbids. It also buys nothing here: a customer's German words pass the quote check unchanged, so the gate was never what excluded other languages. Only the compiler-corroboration requirement is retired, and only where the compiler read *nothing* for that field — where it read something different, the disagreement is still asked about. Unchanged: no model reaches a verdict, `decide()` stays pure, mandates remain append-only and tighten-only, an unsupported rule still never approves, and a run keeps its mandate snapshot. | Product owner, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |

## A card with no history at all is a missing fact — 2026-09-25

Measured against the hosted platform on 2026-09-25: its pack (`saw26-hackaton-api`) ships the same
`authorization_history.csv` as the practice pack (identical md5), and **none of its ten scenario cards
appear in it** — `CA1331`, `CA1022`, `CA1471` and the rest have zero rows, and its twenty new merchants
appear in no history row. A live event carries no familiarity field either, so absence of history was
the only thing our familiarity check could see.

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-046 | **No purchase history at all for a card is a missing fact, not established unfamiliarity.** `familiar_rule` now distinguishes two cases. A card that shows approved purchases somewhere — in history or earlier in the run — and none at this merchant still **fails** the rule (`unfamiliar_merchant`): the evidence exists and says this is not one of their shops. A card that shows no approved purchase anywhere gets a **warn** (`merchant_history_unknown`), which follows the uncertainty policy: `step_up` under `ask`, `decline` under `decline`, and it can never approve on its own. This applies "missing is not permission" in the direction the rule already stated — a missing fact leads to the uncertainty policy, and a `step_up` is not a pass. Without it, every hosted scenario whose instruction mentions shops the customer uses (`SCEN0122`, `SCEN0106`, `SCEN0135`, `SCEN0117`, `SCEN0104`) declines its first purchase and can never build familiarity, because only approvals do — which the brief calls a failure in its own right ("blocking ordinary shopping unnecessarily is also a failure"). Verified on a real hosted event (`AU10001-52a51c16`, Rhine Fresh `ME0102`, CHF 18.00, card `CA1331`): `decline`/`unfamiliar_merchant` before, `step_up`/`merchant_history_unknown` after, with every other check unchanged. The practice pack is unaffected — its cards do have history, and all 45 replay verdicts are byte-identical, including the `unfamiliar_merchant` declines in SCEN0003 and SCEN0004. Open with Viseca: whether the hosted pack's empty history for these cards is deliberate or a placeholder to be replaced on the day. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Proposed |

## The permission assistant when the model is down — 2026-09-25

Found by the independent review of LEASH-175 on 2026-09-25: the built surface returns an error where
LEASH-175's criterion 5 and LEASH-101's ticked criterion both say "questions". The code was deliberate
and undocumented, so the decision is recorded here and the two tickets are corrected to match it.

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-047 | **An unavailable permission model is a retryable error, not a clarification.** When the model times out, errors or returns something unreadable, the assistant surface answers `503` with the failure code (`model_unavailable`, `model_invalid_response`) and writes **nothing**: no draft is created, no turn is appended, `policy.calls` stays empty. The chat shows the failure where it happened and the customer retries. Chosen over the earlier "fall back to a safe clarification flow": a fallback would have to either write a draft carrying no rules — which reads to a customer as "nothing is restricted", the exact failure "missing is not permission" forbids — or ask questions against no draft, so the customer's answers are recorded nowhere, which breaks LEASH-145's rule that the transcript never claims work the backend has not recorded. Falling back to the deterministic compiler was also rejected here: DEC-045 retired it as the authority gate precisely because it reads no German, French or Italian, so a silent downgrade would quietly return the customer to the parser that could not read their words. The safety properties are unchanged and this is why the error is safe: an unavailable model cannot produce a rule, cannot loosen a mandate and cannot reach a verdict — `decide()` never sees this path. Two things stay required: the failure is always visible to the customer (never silence), and no empty draft is ever created. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |

## The `says` gate carries the value for numbers only — 2026-09-25

DEC-045 requires a rule to quote, word for word, something the customer typed, "and that quote must
carry the rule's value". The built reading narrows the second half. Recorded after the LEASH-175 review
found code and decision log disagreeing.

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-048 | **The verbatim-quote gate stays whole; its value-carrying half applies to numeric fields only.** A rule must still quote something the customer actually typed, in the turn it is attributed to — that is what stops a model laundering a background profile line into permission (DEC-034), and it is unchanged. What narrows is the second requirement: for a numeric field (an amount, a quantity, a period, a number of days) the quoted words must still contain the number, because a wrong number is the failure a customer cannot see in a rendered sentence. For a text field (`delivery`, `electronics`, a merchant category) the value need no longer appear literally in the quote: a customer who writes "schick es mir nach Hause" never types the token `delivery`, so requiring it would exclude the ordinary phrasing DEC-045 exists to admit. Such a rule is kept with `evidenced=False` and carries a question, so it is shown to the customer as unconfirmed rather than presented as something they said. This is a narrowing of evidence strength, never of authority: an unevidenced rule still restricts, still cannot loosen anything, and still needs the customer's confirmation to become a mandate. Compensating control: the review must show which rules are evidenced and which are not (LEASH-146), which is already required by DEC-045. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## Two questions the customer cannot act on — 2026-09-25

Both found by reading live chat transcripts rather than tests, because both are true statements about the
assistant's own proposal and false statements about the draft the customer is looking at.

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-058 | **"Unconfirmed suggestion" and "I left it out of the draft" must not be asked about a rule the draft already holds.** The policy service compiles the customer's instruction itself, so every rule the deterministic compiler reads is in `hard_rules` whatever the model did — verified with no model rules supplied at all. Two questions ignore that. (a) A model rule whose quote fails the `says` gate is offered as an unconfirmed suggestion even when the compiler read the identical rule from the customer's own words: live, "One purchase" — words the customer never wrote — was offered as a suggestion while DEC-013 had already read `max_count <= 1` from "Buy one ordinary grocery item", so the draft showed the rule as theirs *and* asked whether they wanted it. **Fixed**: such a suggestion is dropped, since the rule reaches the draft either way. (b) The omission net's "You also said something about X, which I left out of the draft" is raised for every compiler reading the model did not independently produce — each of which is in the draft. That statement is untrue and it blocks a well-read English instruction with one question per rule. **Now fixed** (product owner, 2026-09-25, after seeing it block the same instruction twice and finding that typing "no need be a rule" could not clear it): the question is gone. The same defect was then found at three further branches and fixed with one shared predicate, `_read_by_the_engine`: an unconfirmed suggestion, "I read X as Y — those are my words", and "Which exact catalogue product do you want?" were each asked about a rule the compiler had read from the same sentence, so the draft already showed it as the customer's own. A fourth was the model's own `turn_id`: it tagged a rule to the acknowledgement turn while quoting the instruction, and trusting that claim turned "yes i mean that" into instruction text — a claimed turn now counts as read only when the quote is the customer's words in that turn. Measured after all four, live against Apertus: every one of the five supplied scenarios reaches `ready` with no blocking questions and 27 of the 30 agreed rules, against 13 of 30 with every draft blocked before this work. What it used to convey that was real — *who* read a rule — is in the review's own labels instead (LEASH-146), where a reading from the customer's words is theirs and a default of ours is ours. One consequence recorded deliberately: the "the model returned no proposal" guard now fires only when **neither** reader read anything, because the compiler's readings alone are a real draft the customer can consent to. The five dimensions the old cross-check covered (negation, quantity, purchase count, item kind, shop familiarity) are still pinned, asserted against the draft's own `hard_rules` rather than against a question. The half of the net that is real — a sentence *neither* reader turned into a rule, which has no field in the draft and nothing else can show — is unaffected either way. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |
| DEC-059 | **A turn that states no boundary is an acknowledgement, not an instruction.** "yes", "i am go with that now" were appended verbatim to the draft's `instruction` — the text the mandate carries to the platform — burned a revision each, and left a blocking question the grammar could never read; answering it appended more unreadable words, so the loop tightened the harder the customer tried. Such a turn now leaves the draft untouched and is recorded as a chat message instead, with a reply that says nothing changed and names where the Confirm action is (typing "yes" is what a customer does when they cannot see one). Two independent readers must both find no boundary — no rule the model attributed to that turn, none the compiler reads from it — and only on a continuing turn: the opening words are the instruction by definition. Open questions are deliberately not consulted; on a later turn they are almost always about what was said earlier, and treating them as substance made "yes" an instruction again. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |

## What the permission corpus may treat as inconclusive — 2026-09-25

Found while investigating why `necessary_clarification` sat at 12/50 across v4, v5 and v6 regardless
of how the training sample was weighted. Three defects in the gold, one taxonomy and two mechanical.
All of the corpus work below is diagnostic: no case has been human reviewed and no run is release-gated.

| ID | Decision | Source | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DEC-049 | **`not_stated` and `ambiguous` are separated by what the customer pointed at.** In question banks v1 and v2 the two definitions overlapped — "no current customer instruction establishes this condition" and "relevant evidence is missing, conflicting or cannot resolve this condition" are both true of every invented condition — so which label a case carried was decided by its template family, not by a rule: the `context` family used `not_stated`, the `reference` family used `ambiguous`, for the same phenomenon. Bank v3 fixes the definitions rather than the labels: `not_stated` is "the customer said nothing bearing on this condition; background is not consent", `ambiguous` is "the customer pointed at evidence for this condition, but it cannot be resolved or it conflicts", and every support question carries the tie-break explicitly. The rule ratifies the existing labels in all but one family, so this costs a definition and no relabelling: "buy the monitor I chose" points at an unresolved referent (`ambiguous`), "buy clothing" says nothing about a budget (`not_stated`), "I have not decided between X or Y" conflicts (`ambiguous`). v1 and v2 are untouched, so the v2/v3/v4/v5 corpora stay valid. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |
| DEC-050 | **A stated but unverifiable requirement is `supported`, and the clarification carries the rest.** The `quality-unverifiable` family — customer says "the clothing must be good for professional design work", the draft says "Guaranteed suitable for professional design work" — was labelled `ambiguous`. Under DEC-049 that no longer holds: the customer stated the requirement directly and pointed at nothing, so the evidence is neither missing nor unresolvable. The real defect is that the condition is unenforceable, which is the `unsupported_mandate_rule` axis, not textual support, and it is already recorded on the same case as `clarify:other = yes` ("which measurable product features would satisfy this requirement?"). Encoding it twice, inconsistently, taught the model that a plainly stated requirement is inconclusive. Rejected: adding a fifth `unverifiable` label, which would change the criteria set on every support question in every corpus for 12 cases. 12 development labels moved `ambiguous` → `supported`; no other gold label changed. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |
| DEC-051 | **A generation-mode marker is not evidence: truncated cases are judged from their text.** `evidence_complete` was set by the factory as `mode != 'missing_evidence'`, reached `verifier_input`, and therefore became a model input feature; `validate_corpus` then required every label of such a case to be `ambiguous`, and `export_rows` blanketed them a second time. The flag appears nowhere in the engine, the assistant or the contracts; the evaluator turns it into a `missing_relevant_evidence` abstention before the model is asked; and calibration already excluded those rows. So 342 cases taught a shortcut that production never sees and the metric never scores. Worse, the blanket is wrong on its face: in the 52 multi-condition cases the missing message covers one dimension while the other three are stated verbatim in the message that is present, so 156 of their 208 labels were false. `permission_revision_v4.generate` shows it plainly — it labels each anchor `supported`, then overwrites every label two lines later. The 304 truncated **training** cases now carry `evidence_complete: true` with per-condition labels; the truncation lives in the conversation ("the instruction about X is in a message you do not have"), which is where production sees it. The 38 development and calibration cases keep the flag so the abstention path and the reported metrics still compare to v4, v5 and v6. A consequence worth recording: `clarify`/`omission` `ambiguous` existed **only** as this blanket — complete cases produce yes/no — which is why no model has ever emitted one and the metric has never scored one. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |
| DEC-052 | **Training-sample label weight is not the lever for this task; the gold is.** Three runs settle it. v4 weighted every label equally (false support 51, necessary clarification 12, false omission 166). v5 appended 42 whole scenarios, 97% of them carrying their question's majority answer, and the model collapsed to a constant per question kind **in sample** — `supported` on all 233 support questions, `no` on all 420 clarify questions. v6 gave minority labels twice the weight and the bias flipped rather than centred: false omission 390, needless clarification 123, for one extra detection. v7 reversed the scan labels to favour precision and that was worse again — clarification recall 22 → 14, omission detection 34 → 26, needless clarification 34 → 75. Both directions away from equal weight lose to equal weight. What did move was the corpus work of DEC-049 to DEC-051: on **equal** weights, `necessary_clarification` went 12 → 22 with needless clarification unchanged at v4's 34, false support 51 → 48, missed support 18 → 0, and on the held-out challenge set false support 21 → 17 and false omission 40 → 30. The model also recovered its full label vocabulary in sample (`support` emits all four labels, `clarify` both) where v4 emitted two and one. Sampling weight stays at equal; effort moves to the labels. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Accepted |
| DEC-053 | **No permission model is released on unreviewed gold.** All 3414 cases carry `review.status: pending` and `reviewer: null`, and the 6191 support labels share 42 rationale strings of which 19 are attached to more than one label value — the recorded reasoning is template boilerplate and is sometimes plainly wrong for the case it sits on, so it must never be reviewed against. Every comparison in this log, including v4's incumbency, therefore rests on unmeasured label noise. Two blind review sets are prepared and scoreable: 108 support labels stratified by gold label and split, collapsed to the 40 distinct rules behind them, and 48 omission judgements drawn equally from the four gold/model agreement cells so the 239 development "false omissions" are actually looked at. An assistant pass over the support sample is recorded separately (105 agree, 3 disagree, all one mechanical class) and explicitly does not satisfy this gate: a second machine pass over machine-generated labels measures nothing, and several of these labels encode product decisions rather than facts. Until a person adjudicates, every run stays `release_ready: false` and `human_reviewed: 0`. | Team, 2026-09-25 | Proposed |
